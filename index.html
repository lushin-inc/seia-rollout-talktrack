Emily Shaw

The whole rollout, start to finishFerguson · 46:11 · 90 sec
“I send a video to the team introducing myself, why we're doing it, how to think about the assessment, how to approach the assessment. It's all warm and fuzzy — we're going to see what your inherent skill sets are and your opportunities for growth. And then they get links to take the assessment. We give them two weeks. If you want me to give them longer we can, but I prefer the two-week mark, because it's long enough — and after that they just keep forgetting. We stay in touch with you on who has and who has not completed it, about every three days or so. The assessment itself takes an average of 45 minutes online. Some people really overthink it and it takes them an hour and some change, but the average is around 45. And then we compile results, which takes about three business days to get all of that set up in the deck. And I have all this documented for you too, so you don't have to remember it all. Then we schedule that review — we usually schedule that out in the beginning because my availability is crazy, and everybody else's we'd be inviting. So we get that on the book so it's ready right around the three-and-a-half-week mark.”
The only version in either library that covers the communication side — the intro video, the nudge cadence, warning people about the 45 minutes.
The same thing as a timelineTrue Scripts · 31:12 · 60 sec
“From the time we would launch the assessment links to the time where you could do a kickoff is probably six weeks. It takes a while for those assessments just because we give two weeks for them to take it, because it does take 45 minutes online. And salespeople are huge babies about time. We know that. So you're like, fine, you've got two weeks — and inevitably everybody does it on the last day. And then we schedule a three-hour review with the leadership team so that everybody has full transparency on exactly what's going on and exactly what training would need to look like. We would have a plan at that point, once we had the data, that we would present.”
Use this shape to work backwards from the Summit on the 30th.

The exact question, and the hedged answerMurray Allan · 35:14 · 50 sec
“What does the analysis project look like in terms of cost and timeline? And then what does the ongoing coaching after that constitute? — So twelve hundred bucks for the assessment of the three of you. Your commitment is forty minutes; average testing time is forty-two, forty-three minutes each. You and I get together for seventy-five to ninety minutes afterwards to review. I don't know what your training and development plan would look like. I don't really know what we would do. Time, touches, how often we would see each other. For a group your size, I'm normally around three thousand a month, somewhere around there.”
Total precision on part one is what buys the vagueness on part two. Both halves in ninety seconds.
Scope, cost and goals get agreed together — laterMurray Allan · 37:15 · 55 sec
“Our average client relationship is forty-seven months. But it has nothing to do with commitments that you make very early. It has to do with effectiveness over time. If we mutually decided to work together, we would agree on a scope and a cost and a set of goals, and it would probably be between six and twenty-four months. And then nine months in, we're achieving goals and you're saying, this is great. We just tend not to run out of ways to create a positive ROI.”
Duration and structure instead of a figure, and it reframes a long engagement as earned rather than committed to up front.
